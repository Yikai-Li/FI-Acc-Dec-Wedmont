WRDS validation of retirement portfolio allocation¶

November 19, 2026 | Extension of the Tanish/Wedmont implementation audit

We investigate whether the withdrawal-success frontier and the value of conditioning allocation on planned cash flows survive a change in return measurement. This extension replaces reconstructed Treasury returns with CRSP target-maturity Treasury returns, then replaces the remaining core inputs. The analysis is retrospective and follows the public-data results; it is not a new preregistered test.

The object of study is a portfolio rule under specified withdrawals and preferences. These market-return data contain no household age, reported risk tolerance, or household balance sheets. Accordingly, this analysis cannot estimate the population relationship between age and risk tolerance or financial capacity. The earlier household-survey work addresses a different empirical question.

Data sources and selection¶

CRSP Treasury and Inflation Indexes (crsp.mcti) provide Treasury target-maturity returns, a one-month bill return and CPI inflation. crsp.msix.vwretd supplies the US value-weighted equity return including distributions. These extracts end in December 2024. Treasury return histories start in 1941; the common long calibration begins in May 1953, the first month available for the public Treasury reconstruction.

The CRSP methodology selects a representative issue near each target maturity. It is an observed security-return series assembled under an index methodology, rather than the project's yield-based par-bond reconstruction. It also differs from IEF, which holds a portfolio of securities with maturities of seven to ten years. This choice tests the sensitivity to Treasury measurement without treating the two definitions as identical. See the CRSP data descriptions, section 3.4.

The ETF extract uses crsp.msf_v2.mthret, with distributions included, and ends in December 2025. Each requested ticker resolves to one PERMNO during the extracted period. Ten funds cover international and emerging equities, intermediate and long Treasuries, investment-grade and high-yield credit, TIPS, real estate, gold and commodities. Published inception dates restrict the extraction to the intended funds. First fund months may be partial; none enters the matched core frontier experiment. Portfolio comparisons start in December 2004 for the Permanent Portfolio and May 2007 for the full universe, after the youngest required fund's first partial month.

WRDS Bond Returns supplies a supplementary credit aggregation, August 2002 to May 2026. We use immediately previous-month ratings and amount outstanding, exclude gaps in consecutive months, and retain bonds with a nonmissing current return and positive lagged weight. IG uses rating numbers 1-10; HY uses 11-21. These are lagged par-amount-weighted research portfolios, not market-value-weighted investable indexes. Missing returns and the data provider's bond coverage can affect them. The canonical panel selects TRACE-derived IG and the observed HYG fund for HY under its source-priority rule; the supplementary TRACE HY aggregate is not used by the reported full-universe allocation study.

Raw licensed observations remain local and excluded from Git and the report package. This notebook reads only aggregate results and coverage metadata. Sources: WRDS CRSP product documentation, WRDS Bond Returns product description.

Matched source experiment¶

We run three panels over exactly the same calendar windows: the public baseline, a panel replacing only Treasury returns with CRSP, and a panel replacing Treasury, equity, cash and inflation with CRSP. The Treasury-only comparison isolates that source change; the all-CRSP comparison changes four inputs jointly. No observations are spliced across providers, and missing values are never filled.

Each frontier uses 10,000 bootstrap evaluation paths, 5,000 training paths, seed 20261119, annual rebalancing, a 0%-100% bond grid in 5-percentage-point steps, and annual real withdrawals of 3%-6% of initial wealth. The horizon is 30 years for both success and utility calculations. Trading costs are zero in this matched experiment. A Student-t generator provides an additional scenario comparison.

Success means fully funding all planned withdrawals over the horizon. The minimum bond share is the lowest tested allocation reaching 90% success. An empty value means that no allocation on the grid reaches that target. The frontier is not assumed monotone: a zero minimum does not imply every larger bond allocation is feasible. Bootstrap threshold intervals use 300 resamples of simulation paths and condition on the fitted return generator. They do not include uncertainty about the historical sample, regimes, costs or future market structure. Maximizing reported success across the evaluation grid is descriptive; the separately trained objective comparison below provides independent policy evaluation.

Age, cash flows and independent evaluation¶

The signed-flow surface fixes an exogenous annual net draw as a fraction of initial reference wealth; negative values denote contributions. We fit allocations by age and flow using 4,000 paths and evaluate on 5,000 separate paths, calibrated on January 1953-December 2024 CRSP returns. CRRA risk aversion is 4. The objective is certainty-equivalent terminal real wealth plus cumulative funded withdrawals, with a USD 1,000 floor, rather than a direct measure of household capacity or consumption utility. The age-only rule averages normalized training objectives equally over the declared flow grid. Joint path resampling preserves dependence across age/flow cells.

The 20-year surface gives ages 25-75 the same effective horizon. With zero labor income in the surface experiment, age has no independent cash-flow channel there. Age 85 has a 10-year horizon because the terminal-age cap is 95. Identical fitted allocations for ages 25-75 follow from this model specification.

For historical evaluation, training uses only pre-split returns. Frozen allocations are scored on all completed later 10- and 20-year cohorts through December 2024. The 1990, 2000, 2005, 2010 and 2015 splits are retained only when the full horizon is available. Public and CRSP versions use the same end date. Cohorts and splits overlap; their results are reported separately without an IID standard error or a pooled significance claim. Policies are fixed through each cohort, so this test evaluates static conditional allocations, not annual adaptive retraining. Both historical comparisons begin training in May 1953. The separate objective-selection experiment uses the January 1953-December 2024 CRSP sample, 5,000 training paths and 10,000 evaluation paths.

Expanded portfolios and reproducibility¶

Observed gold, TIPS, high-yield and commodity funds now permit the full multi-asset study and the classic Permanent Portfolio to execute. The Permanent Portfolio uses the existing annual 15/35 band review and the same scenario paths as its 60/40 benchmark. The full multi-asset analysis uses 12 sleeves over their joint available history, with no backfill before fund inception. Results include the source definitions, costs, common windows, generator and run IDs. Different universes and calibration periods prevent a causal attribution of changes to diversification alone.

Interpretation and remaining limits¶

Return measurement changes the estimated spending-success frontier enough to change whether a 90% target is feasible. The result also changes across historical windows. A universal bond allocation or withdrawal guarantee is unsupported by these experiments. The age-plus-flow comparison must be judged from its independent-path and chronological results above; separating the concepts of willingness and capacity is a modeling premise, not an empirical finding from these market data.

All simulations condition on historical calibration, the withdrawal convention, CRRA objective, rebalancing rule and terminal-age cap. Bootstrap and Student-t experiments do not eliminate structural model risk. Historical tests use revised data extracts, overlapping cohorts and selected windows. Monte Carlo intervals measure simulation uncertainty. Country effects, cohort effects, human-capital risk and changes in household preferences remain outside this return-based extension.
